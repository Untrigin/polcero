We have a prototype transport drone with up to 500 kilograms of payload. We don't call it "the 500 kg drone" - payload is a spec, not a name. It is a transport drone, and the number only says how much it lifts.

We see two uses for it today. The first is transport from hard-to-reach places: mountainous and forested terrain, construction sites without paved roads, areas cut off after a failure or flood. The second is carrying loads and people where access is costly, slow or impossible.

## In time, the drone carries robots

The most interesting part is still ahead of us. We want the drone to become a means of transport not only for goods and people, but for our robots. A robot picks up the task modules it needs for a specific job, boards the drone and flies to the site.

This is the missing link of the hourly-rental model. If a robot is to work at a client for a few hours and then at the next one, the cost and time of delivering it decide the economics of the whole service. A drone delivering the robot to the client's site removes the need to maintain a fleet of vehicles and drivers, and frees delivery from traffic and road conditions.

Work on this machine currently focuses on control, autonomy and task equipment. A machine of this class is subject to separate certification requirements, which we treat as part of the schedule, not a detail to skip.
